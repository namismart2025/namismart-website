Eventually, almost every camera owner asks the same question: "how far back does my footage actually go?"

It usually comes up at the worst possible time — a package was stolen four days ago and the footage is already gone, or an insurance adjuster wants video from three weeks back and the system only holds two. Searches asking exactly this — how long security cameras keep footage — have spiked recently, right alongside a surge in searches for cloud-based camera systems. People are trying to understand storage before they get burned by it, not after.

This isn't the same question as "should I pay for a subscription," which we've [covered in detail elsewhere](/blog/security-cameras-no-subscription-la-homeowners). This is narrower and more practical: once you've decided how footage gets stored, how long does it actually stick around — and which approach, cloud or local, gives you the retention you actually need?

## Storage Duration Depends on Three Things

Regardless of cloud or local, how long footage lasts comes down to the same three variables:

**Storage capacity.** For cloud, this is whatever your plan includes. For local, it's the size of the hard drive in your NVR or the SD card in your camera.

**How much data each camera generates.** Higher resolution and higher frame rates mean bigger files. A 4K camera can generate three to four times the data of a 1080p camera recording the same scene.

**Recording mode.** Continuous recording captures every second, all day, every day — and fills storage fastest. Motion-triggered recording only saves clips when something happens, which can stretch the same storage across months instead of weeks.

Change any one of these and the "how long" answer changes with it. That's why there's no single correct number — only a calculation based on your specific setup.

## How Cloud Storage Retention Actually Works

Cloud plans are built around a fixed retention window, not a fixed amount of storage. You're not really buying gigabytes — you're buying days.

Most residential cloud plans fall into a few common tiers:

- **Free tier (if offered):** Often just event clips, sometimes as little as 24–72 hours
- **Basic paid plan:** Commonly 7–14 days of rolling history
- **Standard paid plan:** Commonly 30 days
- **Extended plans:** 60 days or more, usually at a higher monthly cost, sometimes per-camera

Once you hit the edge of that window, the oldest footage disappears automatically — there's no way to extend it after the fact without upgrading the plan going forward. If you didn't catch an incident within your retention window, cloud footage from that moment is simply gone, upgrade or not.

The appeal of cloud is that this retention is predictable and guaranteed by the provider, and the footage survives even if the camera itself is stolen or destroyed. The downside is that predictability comes with an ongoing bill, and the window is usually shorter than most people assume until they actually go looking for old footage.

## How Local Storage Retention Actually Works

Local storage — an NVR with an internal hard drive, or a camera with an SD card — works completely differently. There's no fixed number of days built in. Instead, the system fills the drive and then loops, overwriting the oldest footage to make room for new recordings, continuously, forever.

That means retention is really a math problem: drive size, divided by how much data your cameras generate per day, equals roughly how many days of footage you'll have before it starts overwriting.

Here's what that looks like in practice, using continuous recording as a baseline:

| Setup | Storage | Continuous Recording | Motion-Only Recording |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 cameras, 1080p | 2TB HDD | ~6–8 weeks | ~4–6 months |
| 4 cameras, 4K | 2TB HDD | ~2–3 weeks | ~6–10 weeks |
| 8 cameras, 4K | 4TB HDD | ~2–3 weeks | ~6–10 weeks |
| 8 cameras, 4K | 8TB HDD | ~4–6 weeks | ~3–5 months |

These numbers are estimates — actual retention varies with compression settings, how much motion is in each scene, and bitrate. But the pattern holds: resolution and recording mode matter more than most people expect, and a bigger drive is a straightforward way to buy more retention without paying anything monthly.

The advantage of local storage is that you control the trade-off directly. Want six months of history instead of six weeks? Add a bigger drive, or switch from continuous to motion-triggered recording. There's no plan to upgrade and no recurring cost either way.

## Which One Actually Gives You What You Need?

For most homeowners, the honest answer is that local storage gives you more retention for the money, and cloud storage gives you more protection against the camera itself being compromised.

**If your main concern is "how far back can I look," local wins.** A modest hard drive in an NVR will almost always out-retain a basic cloud plan, often by months instead of weeks, with no ongoing cost after installation.

**If your main concern is "what if the camera gets stolen or destroyed," cloud has a real edge.** Local footage lives with the hardware. If someone takes the camera or the NVR, the recordings usually go with it.

**The setup we install most often for homeowners who want both** is local recording as the primary system — everything captured, stored for months, no subscription — with the NVR kept inside the home in a secure, less obvious location rather than mounted next to the cameras it's recording. That alone solves most of the "what if it gets stolen" concern without paying for cloud storage on every camera, every day.

Some homeowners add cloud backup selectively — specific cameras, or specific triggered events — as a middle ground. You get the deep local archive plus an offsite copy of the moments most likely to matter, without a full cloud subscription across the whole system.

## Why This Matters More in LA Than People Expect

A few things about how people actually use camera footage in Los Angeles make retention length a bigger deal than it first appears.

**Package theft and porch incidents often aren't discovered same-day.** Someone traveling, a package that sat for a few days before anyone noticed it missing, a dispute that only comes up a week later — by the time you go looking, a short retention window has often already erased the relevant footage.

**Insurance claims and HOA disputes move slowly.** It's common for an adjuster or a neighbor dispute to reference an incident from two or three weeks earlier. A 7-day cloud plan won't reach back that far. A local system sized correctly usually will.

**Multi-camera properties fill storage faster than single-camera setups.** A gated home with cameras on the driveway, front door, backyard, and garage generates several times the data of a single doorbell camera — which means retention planning matters more, not less, as a system grows.

## FAQ

### How long do security cameras actually keep footage?

It depends entirely on the storage setup, not the camera itself. Cloud plans typically keep footage for a fixed window — commonly 7, 14, 30, or 60 days — then delete it to make room for new clips. Local storage (an NVR or SD card) keeps footage until the drive fills up, then automatically overwrites the oldest recordings. A local system with a large hard drive recording only motion events can hold months of footage; the same drive recording continuously in 4K might only hold a couple of weeks.

### Is cloud storage safer than local storage for security footage?

Safer against theft or physical damage, yes — if your camera or recorder is stolen, cloud footage is still sitting on a server somewhere. Local storage is gone if the recorder goes with it. But cloud storage isn't safer against everything: it depends on the provider staying in business, your subscription staying active, and their servers staying online. Local storage puts you in control, with the trade-off of no offsite backup unless you add one yourself.

### How much storage do I need for continuous 24/7 recording?

For a rough estimate: a 1080p camera recording continuously uses about 8–12 GB per day with modern compression, while a 4K camera can use 30–40 GB per day. Multiply by your camera count and desired retention window. A 4-camera 1080p system on a 2TB drive typically holds 6–8 weeks of continuous footage; the same setup in 4K might only hold 2–3 weeks. Motion-only recording stretches that considerably, sometimes to several months.

### Can I have both cloud and local storage at the same time?

Yes, and it's one of the more common setups we install. The NVR records everything locally with no subscription required, while select cameras or specific triggered events also back up a clip to the cloud. You get full local retention as the primary record, plus offsite protection for the moments that matter most — without paying a monthly fee for every second of footage.

### What happens when local storage fills up?

Nothing dramatic — the system automatically overwrites the oldest footage to make room for new recordings, the same way a dash cam works. This is standard behavior for both NVRs and SD-card cameras. If you need to preserve a specific clip (for an insurance claim or a police report, for example), you export and save it before it gets cycled out.
